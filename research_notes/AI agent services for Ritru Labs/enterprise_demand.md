## 1. What do 2025-2026 surveys and analyst reports say about agent adoption rates, budgets and top use cases in IT/cloud/DevOps/security?

### Takeaway
Adoption numbers depend heavily on what counts as adoption. Independent analysts put real deployment of agents at roughly 17-23% of organisations: Gartner's 2026 CIO survey says 17%, and McKinsey says 23% are scaling an agentic system. Surveys of large enterprises (KPMG, Deloitte, PagerDuty) report 50-55% "deployed", but those samples are skewed toward large companies and count loosely. Across sources, IT, IT service management and software engineering are consistently the functions where agents are scaled first. Security operations (SOC) agents are at the peak of the hype cycle, with only 1-5% market penetration.

### Cited Findings
**Adoption rates (independent analysts and Big-4 firms)**

- FinOps has a new and almost universal mandate (98% manage AI spend), and its practitioners lack AI-cost skills. That is a services gap more than a gap in agent products.

## 2. Which use cases show measurable ROI in production? (numbers where available)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced ROI is in **large-scale code migration and modernisation**, which has first-party engineering papers from Google and Amazon, and in **gen AI for ITSM** (Gartner's top I&O success area). **Incident triage and root-cause analysis** shows credible gains from first-party operators: Google SRE reports a 10-44% reduction in time-to-mitigate. Headline MTTR claims of 40-70% are mostly vendor-reported, and fully autonomous remediation and self-healing is specifically where projects fail.

### Cited Findings
**Code migration / modernisation (strongest evidence)**
- Google, "Migrating Code At Scale With LLMs At Google" (FSE 2025 industry paper): 39 migrations over 12 months; 595 code changes, of which about 74% and about 69% of edits (two measures) were LLM-generated. Developers *estimated* a 50% reduction in total migration time versus earlier manual migrations. That is an estimate, not a measurement. An earlier Google preprint on the Ads int32-to-int64 migration reported 80% of code modifications were AI-authored, and humans often had to revert incorrect changes. — [arXiv 2504.09691](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09691); [The Register (Jan 2025, older)](https://www.theregister.com/2025/01/16/google_ai_code_migration/)
- Amazon internal (Dec 2024, older data): upgrading tens of thousands of applications to Java 17 with Amazon Q Developer transformation saved more than 4,500 developer-years (Amazon-claimed). — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241203826497/en)
- AWS Transform **[vendor-claimed]**: at its one-year milestone it reported 810 developer-years saved, 4.5B lines of code processed and 1.6M hours saved, plus a claim that a 250,000-line mainframe app can be transformed and tested in about 6 weeks. The current product page claims 1,305 developer-years saved. Customer examples: Thomson Reuters moving 1.5M LOC per month; Signaturit's .NET-to-Linux migration cut from 6-8 months to "a few days". Up to 5x faster full-stack Windows modernisation is claimed. — [AWS one-year blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/migration-and-modernization/aws-transform-one-year-milestone/); [AWS Transform page](https://aws.amazon.com/transform/); [Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news-updates/aws-transform-has-saved-335-developer-years-of-work-in-the-last-12-months/)

**ITSM / ticket resolution**
- Gartner (n=782 I&O leaders): **53% of reported I&O AI successes are gen AI in ITSM**. Success is attributed to integrating AI into existing workflows and to executive support. — [Gartner press release, 2026-04-07](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-07-gartner-says-artificial-intelligence-projects-in-infrastructure-and-operations-stall-ahead-of-meaningful-roi-returns)
- ServiceNow customer claims **[vendor / vendor-adjacent blogs, not verified]**: CANCOM reports 80% ticket deflection across departments with ServiceNow AI Agents, and the City of Raleigh reports 98% deflection on employee requests. ServiceNow reportedly cites 45-60% Virtual Agent deflection as achievable. Now Assist reportedly saves 4-6 minutes per use for ITSM agents and 12-16 minutes for CSM agents, with resolution-note drafting about 80% faster. Commentators stress that results depend on CMDB, runbook and knowledge-base quality. — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/servicenow-ai-accuracy); [enjo.ai buyer's guide](https://enjo.ai/post/servicenow-ai-agents-in-2026-the-honest-buyers-guide); [btrnsfrmd](https://btrnsfrmd.com/blog/servicenow-ai-agents-now-assist-itsm-2026/)

**Incident triage / RCA / AI SRE**
- Google SRE (first-party operator): the "Incident Hypothesis" feature cut Mean Time to Mitigate by about 10%, and Investigation Dashboards cut it by about 44% for supported incidents. — cited in [Ajay Kumar AIOps 2026 analysis](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/aiops-2026-incident-response-agents-mttr/) (secondary; I could not reach the Google primary)
- Datadog Bits AI SRE **[vendor-reported]**: Mondelēz cut P3 incident MTTR from 23 hours to 9 hours and reduced incident volume by 76%. — [Augment Code guide](https://www.augmentcode.com/tools/ai-incident-management-software) (secondary)
- An evaluation guide puts the average MTTR reduction from AI incident management across customers at **17.8%**, which is much more modest than the 40-70% marketing claims. — [Augment Code](https://www.augmentcode.com/tools/ai-incident-management-software) **[vendor]**
- Baseline: the State of Enterprise Reliability 2026 dataset (80,743 incidents, 360 companies) has a median MTTR of 101 minutes, and only 34.9% of incidents are resolved within an hour. — [StackGen](https://stackgen.com/blog/10-sre-best-practices-for-reducing-mttr-in-2026) **[vendor]**
- Benchmarks: SREGym (90 live problems) scores frontier agents at about 72.6% diagnosis and 60.7% end-to-end resolution, falling to 62.6% and 53.7% with realistic telemetry noise. ITBench is reported at about 47% and "hard on-call" at about 10%. — [Ajay Kumar analysis](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/aiops-2026-incident-response-agents-mttr/) (independent blogger, secondary)

**SOC / alert triage**
- Gartner predicts only 15% of SOCs piloting agents will see measurable improvements *without structured evaluation* (see Q1). Independent analysis notes vendor accuracy claims of about 98% against 23-34% on benchmarks, and that essentially no teams let SOC agents act alone. — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/09/prophet-security-evaluating-ai-soc-agents/); [Ajay Kumar AI SOC analysis](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/ai-soc-agents-security-operations-2026/) (secondary)
- ISC2: 70% of security teams that adopted AI tools report positive effects on effectiveness (self-reported, not quantified ROI). — [ISC2](https://www.isc2.org/Insights/2025/07/2025-isc2-ai-pulse-survey)

**Coding broadly**
- Menlo: coding is the "killer use case", at about $4B of departmental spend in 2025 and half of developers using AI daily. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)

**Back-office**
- MIT NANDA: the biggest measured ROI came from back-office automation (cutting BPO and agency spend), not the sales and marketing tools that take most budgets. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)

### Inferences
- Bounded, verifiable, high-volume work (code migrations with test suites, ITSM ticket classification, routing and resolution notes, incident triage and RCA summaries) is where ROI is provable. Open-ended autonomous action (auto-remediation, autonomous SOC response) is where it fails.
- For a consultancy, "migration factory" engagements (Java, .NET, mainframe, IaC upgrades) using hyperscaler tooling such as AWS Transform have the clearest ROI story, and hyperscalers co-fund them (inference; funding programmes were not researched here).
- A credible MTTR claim to put in front of clients is 10-45% on supported incident types, not 50-70%.

### Gaps
- I found no independent (non-vendor) quantified ROI study for agentic FinOps or cost optimisation, or for compliance-evidence automation. Both are absent from analyst data I could reach.
- The Google SRE figures were seen only via secondary citation.
- The ServiceNow deflection figures are vendor or partner blogs; ServiceNow's official case studies were not reachable.

## 3. What fraction of agentic AI projects are predicted to be cancelled or fail, and why?

### Takeaway
Gartner expects more than 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end-2027, because of cost, unclear value and weak risk controls. Gartner's own I&O data (April 2026) shows only 28% of AI use cases in infrastructure and operations fully meet ROI expectations, and 20% fail outright. MIT NANDA's widely cited "95% no P&L return" comes from a small, preliminary sample and applies to genAI pilots generally.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner (June 2025): more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 because of escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls. Analyst Anushree Verma: most projects are early experiments or POCs "driven by hype and often misapplied", and many use cases sold as agentic don't need agents. Only about 130 of the "thousands" of agentic AI vendors are genuine, the rest being "agent washing" (rebranded assistants, RPA and chatbots). — [CRN India](https://www.crn.in/?p=72052); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/artificial-intelligence/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2027-says-gartner); [OODA Loop](https://oodaloop.com/briefs/technology/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2027-gartner-says)
- Gartner I&O survey (n=782, Nov-Dec 2025): **only 28% of AI use cases in I&O fully succeed and meet ROI expectations; 20% fail outright**; the rest deliver partial value. 57% report at least one failure to integrate AI into operations. Research director Melanie Freeze blames inflated goals, and cites **auto-remediation and self-healing infrastructure** as tasks AI is not yet equipped for. Skills gaps and poor data quality were each cited by 38%. "AI that doesn't fit into the organization's operations simply can't deliver ROI." — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-07-gartner-says-artificial-intelligence-projects-in-infrastructure-and-operations-stall-ahead-of-meaningful-roi-returns); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/04/07/only-28-of-ai-infrastructure-projects-fully-pay-off/5221652); [CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/4155396/ai-often-doesnt-deliver-roi-for-it-departments-either.html); [Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/infrastructure-ai-stalls-roi-research-finds)
- Gartner forecast: by 2030, 50% of AI agent deployment failures will be due to insufficient runtime enforcement by AI governance platforms. — via search summary of Gartner material ([Gartner article on agentic oversight](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/agentic-ai-oversight))
- Gartner SOC: 70% of large SOCs are expected to pilot agents by 2028, with only 15% gaining measurable improvement without structured evaluation. — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/09/prophet-security-evaluating-ai-soc-agents/)
- MIT NANDA GenAI Divide (July 2025): 95% of organisations see no measurable P&L return from genAI, and only about 5% of integrated pilots extract millions in value. The cause is a "learning gap": generic tools don't learn from or adapt to workflows. Caveat: the sample is small and preliminary, and "no return" is defined narrowly. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/); [Let's Data Science on methodology](https://letsdatascience.com/news/mit-report-documents-genai-pilot-roi-gap-e0924d7d)
- Counterpoint: Menlo found 47% of AI deals reach production, nearly twice the conversion rate of traditional SaaS. That is measured on *purchased* solutions. — [Menlo](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)
- KPMG Q1 2026: 65% cite difficulty scaling AI use cases (nearly double the prior quarter), and 62% cite skills gaps as a barrier to demonstrating ROI. — [KPMG Q1 2026 blog](https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/blogs/2026/q1-ai-pulse-1.html)
- Unverified figure: an "89% pilot-to-production failure rate" attributed to a "Gartner Agentic AI Pulse 2026" appears in blogs, but I could not verify it. — flagged in [search summary](https://www.channeldive.com/news/agentic-ai-outcome-pricing-models-zendesk-gartner/829209/); do not cite

### Inferences
- The failure pattern is consistent across sources: ambitious autonomy, poor integration into existing workflows, weak data, and no evaluation framework. A services offer built around "evaluation-first pilots" with explicit ROI baselines, plus workflow integration, answers the stated failure causes directly.
- Bought solutions convert to production more reliably than internal builds (Menlo 47%; MIT also found purchased or partnered solutions succeeded more often, per wider coverage). That favours integrating vendor agents over building bespoke agents from scratch.

### Gaps
- I found no independent cancellation-rate data specific to DevOps or security agent projects, only the Gartner I&O and SOC figures above.
- MIT's buy-versus-build success ratio (widely reported as about 67% vs about 33%) did not appear in my results, so it is not cited.

## 4. What are the main adoption blockers?

### Takeaway
In 2026 the blockers shifted from "is the tech ready" to **data readiness and access, identity, permissions and security controls for agents, governance and oversight, and skills**. Trust in vendor guardrails is low, and three-quarters of IT leaders see agents as a new attack vector.

### Cited Findings
- KPMG Q3 2026: the top barriers to agent deployment are data access (59%), security and identity controls (53%), monitoring of AI outputs (47%), policy and guardrail enforcement (42%), and model access management (40%). The label-to-number mapping in the PDF extraction was unclear. — [KPMG Q3 2026 PDF](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/corporate-communications/pdf/2026/AIPulseSurvey_Q3_FINAL.pdf)

- The highest-value gaps are agent identity and least-privilege access (non-human identity), audit trails, eval and monitoring harnesses, and data plumbing to CMDBs, observability and ticketing. These are services-heavy problems, and none is solved by buying another agent.

### Gaps

## 5. How are enterprises buying, who is the buyer, and what role do services firms play?

### Takeaway
Enterprises increasingly **buy rather than build**: Menlo found 76% of AI solutions purchased in 2025, up from 53% in 2024. They mostly take agents embedded in incumbent platforms (ServiceNow, Datadog, PagerDuty, Splunk/Cisco, AWS Transform) and then customise them (85% expect to, per Deloitte). Buying authority is fragmented. CEO or finance sign-off is common, and governance is spread across CIO, CISO, CTO and CDAO. Large system integrators (Accenture, Cognizant, IBM, Infosys, Wipro) are positioning for agentic services, and outcome or fixed-price work is now the majority of Accenture's bookings.

### Cited Findings
**Build vs buy**
- Menlo Ventures 2025: 76% of AI solutions are purchased and 24% built internally, versus 53% purchased and 47% built in 2024. ## 6. Are there signals about outcome-based or per-task pricing for agent services?

### Takeaway
Per-resolution pricing is established in customer-service agents ($0.50-$2 per resolution) and is starting to shape buyer expectations. Gartner data shows outcome-based contracts are still a minority in IT services (about 13-19%). Hybrid models (base fee plus a usage or task tier) dominate in practice. Accenture's 65% fixed-price or outcome-based bookings show the services market moving the same way.

### Cited Findings
- Per-resolution list prices **[vendor pricing, via secondary sources]**: Intercom Fin at $0.99 per resolved conversation; Zendesk at $1.50 per committed automated resolution or $2.00 pay-as-you-go (reportedly down about 50% within a year due to competition); Salesforce Agentforce Help Agent at $2 per autonomous resolution; HubSpot Customer Agent reportedly cut to $0.50 per resolution in April 2026 (from $1.00). — [Channel Dive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/agentic-ai-outcome-pricing-models-zendesk-gartner/829209/); [Pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/post/ai-agent-pricing-models); [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/saas-pricing-is-shifting-from-per-seat-to-usage-and-outcome-what-changes-at-your-next-renewal/)
- Gartner (analyst Tom Coshow, via Channel Dive): only **19% of services buyers and 13% of seller-side service agreements** use outcome-based arrangements today. Gartner projects that through 2031, fewer than 25% of tech services contracts will use outcome-based pricing. — [Channel Dive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/agentic-ai-outcome-pricing-models-zendesk-gartner/829209/)
- A prediction that "by 2030 at least 40% of enterprise SaaS spend shifts to usage-, agent- or outcome-based pricing" is attributed to both Gartner and Deloitte TMT Predictions 2026. The attribution is unclear and unverified. — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/saas-pricing-is-shifting-from-per-seat-to-usage-and-outcome-what-changes-at-your-next-renewal/)
- Hybrid pricing (for example, "$5,000/month including 1,000 tasks, then $2/task") is described as the dominant practical model. Buyers are advised to define "resolution" in the contract, because otherwise vendors control the count. — [Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/blog/why-ai-companies-have-adopted-usage-based-pricing); [Korix](https://korixinc.com/learning-center/ai-pricing-models-2026) **[vendor blogs]**
- AIOps investigation costs are cited at roughly $6.50 per investigation for agentic incident analysis. This is a single independent blogger's estimate. — [Ajay Kumar AIOps 2026](https://www.ajayk.sh/blog/aiops-2026-incident-response-agents-mttr/)
- Services: Accenture's fixed-price and outcome-based work is 65% of FY26 bookings. — [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/tcs-infosys-hcl-tech-wipro-techm-3-takeaways-from-accentures-q4-results-top-picks-559576-2026-10-05)

### Inferences
- In IT, cloud and DevOps, outcome units that clients would recognise include: per migrated application or module (code modernisation); per auto-resolved or deflected ITSM ticket (anchored by the $0.50-$2 customer-service benchmarks); per triaged alert or investigation (about $6.50 cost basis per the blogger estimate); and a share of verified cloud savings (the classic FinOps shared-savings model). A pure outcome model is still unusual (13-19% of contracts), so a fixed-fee pilot followed by an outcome-linked run phase is the low-risk design.
- Falling per-resolution prices (Zendesk down about 50% and HubSpot down 50% within a year) mean outcome units commoditise quickly. Consultancies should anchor on the integration and governance value, not on per-task arbitrage.

### Gaps
- I found no published outcome-based pricing for IT-ops, SRE, SOC or FinOps *agent services* from consultancies. Evidence is limited to customer-service agent pricing and SI aggregate bookings mix.
- Gartner's primary pricing research was not reachable (the Channel Dive fetch was blocked), so the numbers come via search summary.
