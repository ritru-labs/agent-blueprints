# AI/ML Operations Agent Opportunities and Business Model for Agent Services (Ritru Labs, as of Oct 2026)

Method note for the report writer: research was done 11 Oct 2026 using web search (25 tool calls). Direct page fetches failed on several primary domains (aws.amazon.com, help.salesforce.com, orrick.com, assets.kpmg.com returned DNS errors), so many figures below come from search-result summaries of secondary sources (law-firm notes, trade press, vendor blogs). Each bullet is labelled where possible as [Primary], [Independent/press/analyst], or [Vendor marketing / commercially interested]. Treat price points from third-party blogs as indicative and re-check against official rate cards before quoting to clients.

## A1. What enterprises need to run AI agents in production (LLMOps/AgentOps, evals, observability, cost, routing, guardrails, RAG, MCP, identity)

### Takeaway
The independent evidence says the bottleneck is no longer building agent demos. It is getting them into production with measurable value and risk controls: Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by end-2027 for cost, value, or risk-control reasons, and MIT NANDA found about 95% of organizations saw no measurable GenAI return. That gap (evals, observability, cost control, identity, MCP governance) is the "AI ops" service opportunity. The tooling layer is consolidating (ClickHouse bought Langfuse; hyperscalers are adding evals and policy into their agent runtimes), which pushes integrators toward configuration, integration and run-services rather than building tools.

### Cited Findings
**Demand and failure-rate evidence (independent/analyst)**
- Gartner (June 25, 2025) predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 "due to escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls"; analyst Anushree Verma said most current projects are early-stage experiments or PoCs that hide the real cost and complexity of deploying at scale. [Analyst] — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
- Gartner calls the rebranding of assistants, RPA and chatbots without real agentic capability "agent washing" and estimates only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real (an estimate, not a count). [Analyst] — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)

It used 300+ public initiatives, 52 organization interviews and 153 leader surveys. Coverage blames integration/workflow "learning gap" problems, not model quality. Not peer reviewed, and press reports give different sample counts. [Secondary / vendor] — [Toloka](https://toloka.ai/blog/the-future-of-mcp-enterprise-adoption/); [Maxim MCP gateways](https://www.getmaxim.ai/articles/best-mcp-gateways-in-2026-what-they-do-and-how-to-pick-one/)

[Secondary citation of research] — [Akto / OWASP guides](https://www.akto.io/blog/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-ai-security)

### Inferences
- The strongest independent demand signal is the "pilot-to-production gap" (Gartner, MIT NANDA). An offer framed as "production-readiness for agents" (eval harness, tracing, cost budgets, identity, MCP gateway) speaks directly to the reasons Gartner gives for cancellations: cost, unclear value, risk controls.
- Tooling is commoditizing and consolidating: Langfuse is now owned by ClickHouse, and AWS ships Evaluations and Policy as metered features. Ritru should stay tool-agnostic and sell the method (eval datasets, regression gates, dashboards, runbooks) packaged as blueprints, rather than betting on one observability vendor.
- MCP governance (private registry, gateway, per-tool least privilege, server vetting) is a young, standards-backed niche where an open-blueprint firm can credibly lead, because the spec deliberately leaves registry hosting and policy to each organization.
- Agent identity now has GA vendor products (Entra Agent ID, Okta), so integration work is moving from "design your own" to "configure and govern", which suits fixed-scope service packages.

- No independent figures were found on guardrail product adoption (e.g., Bedrock Guardrails, Azure Content Safety) or on RAG pipeline service demand specifically.

## A2. Platforms for building and hosting agents (AWS, Azure, Google, Anthropic, OpenAI) and remaining integrator gaps

### Takeaway
All major vendors now offer managed agent runtimes with consumption pricing, but they are split across clouds, often renamed, and partly in preview or beta. OpenAI is forcing migrations: the Assistants API sunset was Aug 26, 2026, and Agent Builder reportedly closes Nov 30, 2026. Integrators fill the gaps: cross-platform architecture, migration, evals, identity, cost governance and connecting to enterprise systems.

### Cited Findings
- **AWS Bedrock AgentCore:** modular (Runtime, Gateway, Memory, Identity, Policy, Evaluations, Optimization and more; about 12 metered components). Components moved from late-2025 preview to GA through early 2026. Policy GA was Mar 3, 2026 and Evaluations GA Mar 31, 2026; Optimization is still in public preview. Pricing is consumption-based with no minimums. Models, tools and knowledge connections bill separately (file search storage, code interpreter sessions, web search transactions). ### Inferences
- The runtimes are cheap per unit; the real cost and risk sit in tokens, tools and integration. That favors integrators who sell architecture, cost modelling and operations rather than hosting.
- Forced migrations (Assistants API to Responses/Agents SDK, Agent Builder retirement, Google SKU and brand changes) are near-term, fixed-scope "migration sprint" opportunities that suit open blueprints.
- Fast renames and preview/beta status (Managed Agents beta, AgentCore Optimization preview) create a need for a "platform-neutral" advisor that tracks status. A versioned blueprint catalog with "last verified" dates would be a differentiator.

## A3. AI governance demand (EU AI Act 2025-2027, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF) and the readiness-assessment services market

### Takeaway
The EU "Digital Omnibus" (in force July 27, 2026) postponed high-risk AI obligations to Dec 2, 2027 (Annex III) and Aug 2, 2028 (Annex I). Article 50 transparency duties and GPAI obligations were not delayed. ISO/IEC 42001 is growing fast from a small base (reportedly under 500 certified organizations in early 2026), with auditor backlogs and cost ranges that support a readiness-services market. Certification remains voluntary unless contracts require it.

### Cited Findings
- **Digital Omnibus final status:** signed July 8, 2026, published in the Official Journal, in force July 27, 2026. [Law-firm/commentary] — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Orrick, Jul 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)
- **High-risk deadlines:** Annex III standalone high-risk systems (biometrics, employment, credit scoring, education, critical infrastructure) move from Aug 2, 2026 to Dec 2, 2027. Annex I AI in regulated products moves to Aug 2, 2028. One commentary says application is linked to the Commission confirming harmonised standards, with those dates as absolute backstops. [Law-firm/commentary] — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act); [AlpacaX](https://www.alpacax.com/blog/eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-moved-to-2027-what-didnt/)
- **Not delayed:** Article 50 transparency duties have applied since Aug 2, 2026, and GPAI obligations kept their original timeline. Sources conflict on the Art. 50(2) watermarking grace period for systems already on the market (Dec 2, 2026 vs Feb 2, 2027); check the OJ text. The member-state regulatory sandbox deadline moves to Aug 2, 2027. [Commentary] — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Formalize](https://formalize.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus)
- **ISO/IEC 42001 adoption:** fewer than 500 organizations worldwide were reportedly certified as of early 2026 (single vendor estimate). Demand outpaced auditor capacity in 2025-26, and Stage 2 audit waits at some bodies exceeded 6 months in 2025. Certification is not legally required unless a contract or SLA demands it. [Vendor/consultancy blogs] — [Strac](https://www.strac.io/blog/iso-42001-certification); [Bright Defense](https://www.brightdefense.com/news/iso-42006-raises-the-bar-for-iso-42001-certifiers/); [Vanta](https://www.vanta.com/collection/iso-42001/who-needs-iso-42001)
- ISO/IEC 42006 now sets stricter requirements for bodies certifying against 42001. [Consultancy news] — [Bright Defense](https://www.brightdefense.com/news/iso-42006-raises-the-bar-for-iso-42001-certifiers/)
- **ISO 42001 cost and timeline:** 6-12 months, with initial audit $5K-$30K+ (one guide). Schellman (the first ANAB-accredited 42001 certification body) reportedly quotes $20K-$40K for year-1 Stage 1 and 2 audits. Total program cost is put at $180K-$320K for mid-market and $350K-$650K for enterprises with 500+ staff (one consultancy's estimate, including internal effort). [Commercially interested sources] — [Strac](https://www.strac.io/blog/iso-42001-certification); [Elevate Consult](https://elevateconsult.com/insights/iso-42001-certification-vs-compliance-understanding-the-cost-differences-in-2026/); [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/iso-42001)
- Governance tools are mapping the OWASP Agentic Top 10 into governance frameworks, alongside ISO and NIST controls. [Vendor docs] — [Modulos](https://docs.modulos.ai/frameworks/owasp-top-10-agentic)

### Inferences
- The Omnibus delay shrinks the "panic compliance" window for high-risk systems but leaves near-term triggers: Art. 50 transparency (chatbots and agents disclosing that users are talking to AI, content marking) and GPAI downstream duties. A fixed-fee "EU AI Act agent transparency and risk classification" assessment stays sellable in 2026-27, with high-risk conformity work ramping toward Dec 2027.
- ISO 42001 has a small certified base, auditor backlogs and $20K-$40K audit fees, plus a large internal implementation cost. That points to demand for readiness assessments and AIMS implementation help, which a small firm can offer while partnering with an accredited certification body (it cannot certify itself).
- Agent-specific controls (OWASP Agentic Top 10, MCP governance, agent identity) can be packaged as evidence for ISO 42001 Annex A and NIST AI RMF "Manage" functions. This is a natural bridge between Ritru's technical blueprints and governance revenue.

### Gaps
- Primary EUR-Lex / Official Journal text of the Omnibus and artificialintelligenceact.eu timeline were not fetched. The original pre-Omnibus milestones (prohibitions and AI literacy from Feb 2, 2025; GPAI from Aug 2, 2025) were not verified in this session.
- NIST AI RMF 2026 updates (e.g., agent-specific profiles) were not researched. No source was found.
- No independent price benchmarks were found for EU AI Act readiness assessments or ISO 42001 gap assessments specifically.
- No reliable count of ISO 42001 certifications from an accreditation body (ANAB, UKAS) was found.

## B1. How AI agent services and products are priced (2025-2026), with concrete price points

### Takeaway
Agent pricing in 2026 is a mix of seats, consumption and outcomes. Salesforce Agentforce runs about $0.10 per action via Flex Credits, with $2 per conversation still available. Fin (formerly Intercom) charges $0.99 per outcome and $9.99 per sales qualification. Sierra and Decagon are sales-gated, with large annual contracts. Services firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain) are moving fees toward outcome- or risk-based models. Boutique agent work typically uses fixed-fee pilots plus monthly retainers, but public benchmarks come mostly from interested vendors.

### Cited Findings
**Software and agent vendor pricing**

The $2 per conversation model still exists. PayGo and pre-commit purchase options exist. One action covers up to 10K tokens. Sandbox rates are disputed (16 credits vs $0.08). Pay-per-resolution on a "Help Agent" SKU is reportedly launching mid-2026 (unconfirmed). ## B4. Standard safety and production practices for delivering agents to clients

### Takeaway
A rough consensus baseline has formed around the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (Dec 2025): least privilege and "least agency", per-tool scoping, short-lived just-in-time credentials, sandboxed execution, MCP supply-chain controls, human approval for high-impact actions, and full tracing and audit logs. Platform features (AgentCore Policy and Evaluations, Entra Agent ID, MCP gateways) now implement many of these, so delivery standards can be enforced in tooling rather than only in documents.

### Cited Findings
- OWASP GenAI Security Project published the Top 10 for Agentic Applications on Dec 9, 2025, developed by 100+ contributors from real 2025 incidents. [Secondary summaries of a primary standard] — [Teleport](https://goteleport.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/); [Auth0](https://auth0.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications-lessons/); [NHI Mgmt Group](https://nhimg.org/complete-guide-to-the-2026-owasp-top-10-risks-for-agentic-applications)
- The Top 10 separates least privilege (what an agent can access) from "least agency" (how freely it can act on that access). [Secondary] — [Gravitee](https://www.gravitee.io/blog/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-2026-a-practical-review-and-how-gravitee-supports-secure-agentic-architecture)
- **ASI02 tool misuse:** strict per-tool least privilege (scope, rate limits, allowed data). Examples are a read-only CRM tool and a payments tool limited to small refunds. **ASI04 supply chain:** controls for tool-response poisoning, agent-to-MCP-server authentication and least-privilege scopes. **ASI05 code execution:** no network, root or write access unless the task needs it, i.e. sandboxing. [Secondary] — [Akto](https://www.akto.io/blog/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-ai-security); [Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/)
- Credentials should be ephemeral and just-in-time rather than standing access. [Secondary] — [Arnica](https://www.arnica.io/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications)
- Entra Agent ID issues short-lived tokens through blueprints after user or admin consent, which supports least-privilege agent credentials in practice. [Secondary] — [i3solutions](https://i3solutions.com/identity-access-management-solutions/non-human-identity-governance-microsoft-entra/)
- AgentCore Policy (GA Mar 2026) applies authorization checks per agent action, metered per request. AgentCore Evaluations (GA Mar 2026) supports built-in and custom evaluators, which can serve as pre-release and continuous eval gates. [Third-party] — [CloudBurn](https://cloudburn.io/blog/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-pricing)
- MCP gateways enforce per-user and per-tool access control, guardrails and end-to-end tool-call tracing. The MCP roadmap prioritizes audit trails and OAuth 2.1. [Vendor/secondary] — [Maxim](https://www.getmaxim.ai/articles/best-mcp-gateways-in-2026-what-they-do-and-how-to-pick-one/); [Toloka](https://toloka.ai/blog/the-future-of-mcp-enterprise-adoption/)
- Self-hosted observability (e.g., Langfuse) is used to keep traces in-region for EU AI Act logging and data-residency needs. [Integrator blog] — [WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/langfuse-llm-observability-eu-ai-act-logging/)
- Outcome-based pilots work best with the success metric agreed in writing before work starts. [Vendor practice] — [Strataigize](https://www.strataigize.com/insights/how-much-do-ai-agents-cost/)

### Inferences
- Ritru can publish a "production readiness gate" blueprint mapped to the OWASP Agentic Top 10 and ISO 42001 controls. It would cover: an eval suite with regression thresholds before each release; tracing on every tool call; per-tool least-privilege scopes; JIT credentials via the platform's agent identity; sandboxed code execution with no network by default; human approval for irreversible or high-value actions; per-agent token and spend budgets; an incident runbook with a kill switch and rollback; and data-residency choice of region and self-hosted traces. That makes safety both a deliverable and a differentiator.
- Contract terms should mirror these controls. Define client-owned credentials and accounts; Ritru access through least-privilege, time-boxed roles; liability caps tied to fees; explicit exclusion of model-vendor errors; shared incident-response SLAs; and outcome metrics with baseline, measurement method and caps.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found on standard liability, indemnity or contract terms for agent-delivery services (for example typical liability caps, AI-specific indemnities, or hyperscaler and model-vendor IP indemnity pass-through). This needs legal sources.
- No independent source was found that documents incident-handling norms for agent failures (for example an agent-specific incident taxonomy or post-incident reporting expectations). EU AI Act serious-incident reporting applies to high-risk systems on the delayed timeline, but this was not verified here.
- The primary OWASP document was not fetched; risk IDs (ASI02, ASI04, ASI05) and wording come from secondary guides.
